How to set VFR personal minimums

Personal minimums are your go / no-go numbers — often stricter than legal VFR. VFR Buddy turns those numbers into an orange alert on the favorites list so you do not have to decode every METAR from scratch.

What personal minimums are (and are not)

FAA VFR weather minimums are the legal floor. Personal minimums are what you will actually accept for this aircraft, currency, and mission. They are not a clearance and not a briefing. VFR Buddy is a glance layer on NOAA/NWS Aviation Weather Center data.

The four numbers in VFR Buddy

  • Ceiling (ft) — broken/overcast height you will not go below
  • Visibility (SM) — statute miles you want at the field
  • Max wind (kt) — sustained wind you will accept
  • Max gust (kt) — gust limit; critical for PPG and ultralight

Defaults in the app are conservative VFR-style starting points (for example 3,000 ft ceiling and 5 SM). Change them. A paramotor pilot’s gust number will not match a high-performance GA airplane.

A simple way to pick starting values

Start with what you already use on a typical local flight. Add margin for a new airport, night, or rust. If the METAR is below your ceiling, vis, wind, or gust, VFR Buddy paints that airport orange instead of the usual VFR/MVFR/IFR/LIFR color. That is the “personal minimums checker” — not a calculator that tells you to launch.

Then confirm with a real briefing

Tap through for TAF, SIGMET, and NOTAM. Get an official weather briefing before you fly. Color on a phone is situational awareness, not a go decision.
