Simplified Weather for Drone & Remote Pilots

Part 107 remote pilots and FPV flyers need fast, low-altitude weather, not a full IFR briefing. VFR Buddy displays METAR, TAF and NOTAM data including: gusts, ceiling, and visibility in a fast and easy format so you can decide whether conditions are workable before you launch.

Why drone and UAV weather is different

Most drone operations happen below 400 feet AGL, where surface wind and gusts matter more than they do for manned GA traffic at pattern altitude. A steady 12-knot breeze with 18-knot gusts might be fine for a fixed-wing pilot but uncomfortable (or unsafe) for a small drone. VFR Buddy highlights wind and gust values alongside ceiling and visibility so you see what affects your aircraft at the altitudes you actually fly.

FAA Part 107 Weather Requirements

FAA Part 107 requires at least 3 statute miles visibility and cloud clearances that depend on your operating altitude. VFR Buddy displays NOAA AWC weather data for the nearest airports to your operational area.

  • Visibility
  • Ceiling and cloud layers that affect VLOS operations
  • Wind direction and speed for takeoff, landing, and hover stability
  • Gust spread that can push a light UAS off station

Airports below your personal minimums turn orange on the favorites list and home screen widget so you notice them before you drive to the site.

FPV and hobby flyers

FPV and recreational pilots often fly tighter wind envelopes than Part 107 commercial operators. Set gust limits that match your airframe and skill level — many small quads are happiest under 10–15 kt sustained with modest gust spread. Save launch sites and nearby METAR stations as favorites, then check the widget or app before every session.

How to use it before a drone flight

Add the airports or heliports nearest your operating area to favorites (3 on Free, unlimited on Pro). Set personal minimums (on Pro): for Part 107-style ops, start with 3 SM visibility, a ceiling that matches your planned altitude and cloud clearance, and wind/gust limits that fit your UAS. On the Home screen or widget (widget on Pro), scan for orange (below your limits) or red/violet (IFR/LIFR). Tap a location for full METAR, TAF, NOTAM, and SIGMET text. If conditions look acceptable, continue with your normal preflight checklist, LAANC where required, and any official weather briefing your operation demands.
